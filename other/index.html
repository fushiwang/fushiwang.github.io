The Preference Cycle

An economist asks you to rank three things. You ask whether lunch is included. The economist writes down your first preference.

Transitivity makes a simple demand. If you prefer A to B and B to C, you should also prefer A to C. Let’s try it with six choices across two dilemmas.∗

The Referee’s Coin

The coin is fair… or is it? I drew one of three coins from my desk drawer. One lands heads 40% of the time, one 50%, and one 60%. Each was equally likely to get picked.

Flip as often as you like, then tell me whether your coin is fair. As usual, the referee would like one more observation.†

The same coin stays on your desk until you give your verdict.
